A monsoon evening at a window over the Ganga, in Bhagalpur.

Touch the glass. Wipe it, write on it, hold still to breathe on it. Stay a few minutes; the storm comes and goes.

Headphones help. Lightning flashes are soft and can be turned down.

Your browser can't draw the glass, so here is the view from the window, and the poem the rain writes on it.

What the rain writes

  1. गंगा आज ऊँची बह रही है The Ganga runs high tonight.
  2. बिजली ने घाट को एक पल देखा Lightning looked at the ghat, once.
  3. जो लिखा, बारिश ने पढ़ लिया Whatever I wrote, the rain read.
  4. अब दीये बहने दो Now let the lamps float.
  5. फिर बरसेगा It will rain again.

बरसात

Barsaat

A monsoon evening on the Ganga, seen from an upstairs window above a ghat in Bhagalpur, Bihar. The storm takes about three minutes to build, break and clear, then it starts again.

On the glass

  • Drag to wipe the fog. Use more than one finger if you like.
  • Write anything. The rain will read it.
  • Hold still to breathe a fresh cloud onto the pane.
  • Keyboard: arrows move your finger, space breathes, n moves the weather on.

How it's made

Hand-written WebGL2 and GLSL, no engine. Raindrops are simulated one by one and act as tiny lenses. The fog is a simulation that regrows, clears where water runs, and keeps the shape of your finger. Every sound, from the rain to the temple bell, is synthesised live with Web Audio. The river is a single photographic plate, always seen through the glass.
